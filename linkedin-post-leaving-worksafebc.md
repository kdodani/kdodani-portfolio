## Copy-ready post

The hardest product problems I've worked on don't show up on a roadmap. They show up as faxes, phone calls, and workflows people have learned to tolerate.

After a few years at WorkSafeBC, I'm closing out my chapter as Product Manager for provider experience—and looking for my next product role in California.

Healthcare operations changed how I think. Adoption rarely fails because people don't care. It fails when workflow, incentive, and feedback never align. Most of my work has been finding that alignment—through product strategy, UX, experimentation, analytics, and AI—in regulated environments where stakeholders don't report to you.

On the provider portal platform, I helped modernize identity for a system carrying 60,000+ referrals a year. Platform strategy under a fixed deadline: coordinating government, vendors, and clinicians without interrupting critical services.

Provider invoicing was a growth problem disguised as a UX problem. The product worked for one segment and had hit a ceiling. Discovery showed a small set of high-volume providers drove most paper volume. I redesigned submission around their workflows, paired the release with a deliberate adoption strategy, and instrumented funnels so we'd know within days whether a new segment was activating—not just whether we'd shipped.

Interoperability started with a question I'd heard for years: why was clinical data still constrained by a billing spec from 2001? I led discovery, advocated for a direct API path over safer options, secured funding, and validated it—physicians submitting from inside their EMR. We scoped how to close provider uncertainty through confirmation and transparency.

Referral Radar was a zero-to-one sprint to help case managers find referral opportunities buried across claim documents. I shaped the product direction, unblocked validation when production data wasn't available, and pushed the team past a recommendation list toward an experience people could explore.

What ties this together: I question assumptions, take on problems others avoid, and build from first principles. I combine interviews with measurement. I look for leverage. I think in systems.

I've become a PM who wants to reduce administrative burden, modernize platforms others depend on, and design AI-native experiences that support human judgment.

If you're building in healthcare, platforms, or intelligent workflows in California—or know someone who is—I'd love to connect. kdodani-portfolio.vercel.app
